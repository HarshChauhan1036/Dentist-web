A small practice that stayed small on purpose

Seventeen years, one location, four dentists. We've turned down every offer to become a chain because the thing patients like about us is the thing chains lose first.

Our story

Founded on a complaint

In 2009, Dr. Elena Alvarez left a busy corporate clinic where dentists were measured on revenue per chair. She opened Brightleaf with two rooms, a second-hand X-ray unit, and a rule that still holds: nobody here is given a treatment target.

The practice grew by word of mouth — mostly from patients who had been quoted for work they didn't need somewhere else, came here for a second opinion, and stayed. Today we look after just over twelve thousand people from Riverside and the surrounding neighbourhoods.

We've invested the growth back into the building rather than into new branches: an in-house milling unit, a 3D scanner, a quiet room for anxious patients, and appointment slots long enough that nobody feels rushed.

How we work

Four standards we don't bend

These aren't posters in the waiting room. Each one changes something concrete about how an appointment runs.

Least treatment that works

If watching a tooth for six months is a reasonable option, that's the option we lead with. We'll tell you the risk of waiting and let you decide — and we write it in your notes either way.

Appointments that don't run late

We book fewer patients per day than the average practice. It costs us revenue and buys you an appointment that starts on time and doesn't feel like a conveyor belt.

Comfort is clinical, not a perk

Topical numbing before every injection, warmed anaesthetic, headphones, blankets and a raised-hand signal that stops everything immediately. Pain makes people avoid dentists for years.

Skills kept current

Every clinician does at least 40 hours of continuing education a year, and we review each other's complex cases in a Friday morning meeting before treatment starts.

The building

Equipment that shortens your visit

We buy technology for one reason: fewer appointments, less discomfort, more predictable results. If a device doesn't do at least one of those, it doesn't come through the door.

  • 3D CBCT scanner — implant positions planned on screen before surgery, not judged by eye.
  • Chairside milling unit — same-day porcelain crowns, no temporaries.
  • Intra-oral scanner — digital impressions instead of gagging on putty trays.
  • Quiet room — a treatment room with no drill noise carry-over, kept for anxious and first-time patients.
  • Step-free access — ground-floor entry, accessible restroom, and a wheelchair-transferable chair in room two.
